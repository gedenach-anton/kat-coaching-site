Mediation

When talking doesn't solve it anymore

Why it helpsBeneath the issue

Most conflicts I see aren't about the issue on the table. Two people can agree on the goal, want the same outcome, and still be stuck, because each hears something the other didn't say. Mediation slows the conversation down enough that both sides can hear what's actually being said, and what's underneath it.

ScopeWhat I mediate

  • Workplace and team conflicts
  • Disputes between business partners and co-founders
  • Intercultural conflicts, where the same words carry different meanings

I work with companies and with individuals.

ProcessHow it works

  1. Intro call

    A free 30-minute call. You describe the situation, I explain how mediation works, and we see whether it fits your case. If you like, send a short case brief beforehand. It's not required, but it helps us use the time well.

    Mediation is voluntary, so every party involved has to agree to take part.

  2. Separate pre-talks

    I meet each party individually first. Everyone gets space to say what they need to say without being interrupted or defending themselves.

  3. Joint sessions

    Sessions are usually 120 minutes. How many we need depends on the conflict and what's at stake. I don't decide who is right and I don't propose the solution. I lead the process, you build the agreement.

  4. Follow-up

    One month or one quarter later, we meet again to check whether the agreement holds in practice and adjust what doesn't.

BoundariesWhat I don't take on

Cases already in court, and pre-divorce mediation. If that's your situation, I'm happy to point you to colleagues who specialise in it.
